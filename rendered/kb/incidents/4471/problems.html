How a report is placed

  1. One id, one report. The ticket export comes in pages that overlap, so some reports come twice. Each id counts once.
  2. A one-day move is shift. The desk already read some reports as a date moved by one day (desk-notes.json). A month/day flip can’t do that: it moves a date by months, or not at all.
  3. Everything else goes by tenant, not by intake’s tag. causeOf in causes.ts looks at the tenant’s locale, their booking form and the channel the report came in by. Intake’s tag is a guess from the reporter’s browser and is wrong often enough not to count on it.

The problems

parser: every date is read month first

The shared parser reads dates month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first, so any date whose day is 12 or less comes out with day and month swapped: a booking for 7 April lands on 4 July. This is what #4471 describes, and its queued fix is the fix for this problem.

hint: booking-v3 asks for the month first

The booking-v3 form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field, for tenants who write the day first. People type what the hint asks; the form reads it the tenant’s way, day first. The result looks the same as parser, but the parser fix does not touch it: the hint has to follow the tenant’s locale. Only reports sent through the form of a booking-v3 tenant land here.

shift: the date moves by one day

Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday. Same meeting. We held it twice.

These reports describe a date that is one day off, or an invite dated before its booking, never a swapped month. A date that is stored as a moment in time and shown in another time zone does this. It needs its own reproduction, by time zone, and its own fix.

Lessons

  • A subject line says how a report feels, not what broke. Different bugs share “wrong day”.
  • Before calling a merged ticket known, place its reports by cause and count them from the data.
  • Notes the desk already wrote (here: the one-day moves) are evidence. Read them before merging.
